## Leg-band and contact-information lesson: local draft (2026-10-15)

The founder clarified that identification belongs among the first steps after receiving a bird, and the same page should help someone who finds another person's bird. The shared seven-question lesson retains its stable `home/identification` route but is now the second item in `เพิ่งรับน้องมา`, after preparing a cage, for both Cockatiel and Sun Conure. It first covers observing an existing band without forced handling, documenting its code and the bird's history, then optionally discussing a readable name/phone contact band or microchip with an avian veterinarian. The finder path starts with safe, quiet temporary care, then checks for a visible phone, records a code if present, asks a bird-capable vet about a microchip, checks local lost-bird notices, and verifies a claimant with nonpublic identifying details. Most breeder-band codes do not automatically lead to an owner's contact. Do not publish the full band code, fit or remove a band at home, or promise recovery. An urgent leg warning appears before the questions. Both versions remain pending veterinary editorial review; no public release or product claim.
